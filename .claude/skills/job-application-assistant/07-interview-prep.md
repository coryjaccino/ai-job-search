## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. IHG — $77M in Proposed Cost Savings & Revenue Generation (Strategic Analysis)

**S:** As a Performance Marketing Specialist and later Search Analyst at IHG (2009-2011), the company was recovering from the 2008 financial crisis. The Web & Interactive Marketing department needed to maximize every dollar spent while finding new revenue opportunities across 4,400+ global hotel properties.

**T:** Identify and propose avenues for cost savings and revenue generation across the paid search program, while maintaining or improving the ROI of existing campaigns.

**A:** Analyzed global paid search data across all brands and regions to identify inefficiencies, redundant agency work, and untapped opportunities. Created macros and automated Excel tools to reduce manual reporting labor and human error. Collaborated with the Test & Learn team to evaluate Google product betas. Presented detailed proposals with quantified opportunity costs to senior management.

**R:** Proposed over $77 million in total opportunity cost savings and additional revenue generation in 2010 alone. Reduced reliance on high-commission OTA channels. Work was recognized by management as instrumental to the post-recovery growth of the paid search program.

**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you identified a major opportunity", "Describe a time you used data to drive business decisions", "How do you approach cost optimization"

### 2. Turner Broadcasting — 73% CPI Reduction (Performance Optimization)

**S:** As a Google Ads Specialist at Turner Broadcasting (2013-2014), managed a $3M+ yearly advertising budget for new TV shows and promotions across TNT, tbs, and TCM. The TCM mobile app needed more installs within a fixed budget.

**A:** Analyzed campaign performance data to identify underperforming segments and reallocated budget to highest-performing channels and creative combinations. **R:** Reduced CPI by 73% within the first month, dramatically increasing install volume without additional budget. Established a framework for ongoing optimization that was applied to other campaigns.

**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you optimized a campaign", "Describe how you improved a key metric", "How do you approach budget optimization"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave [previous company]?"
> **For consulting-to-full-time transition:** "I've spent 18 years building a consulting practice that serves everyone from startups to Fortune 500 companies. I'm now looking for a role where I can apply that breadth of experience within a focused team environment, collaborate more closely with colleagues, and deepen my impact in a specific domain rather than across many clients simultaneously."

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]."
> "I've earned 14 Google Cloud certifications, 9 Microsoft Azure certifications, and AWS and Oracle certifications — demonstrating the ability to rapidly master new cloud platforms and technologies. More importantly, I've transitioned from pure digital marketing to AI/ML engineering over the past 5 years, which shows I can learn and apply new technical domains quickly. I'd bring that same learning ability to [specific skill]."

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> "I see myself at the intersection of AI/ML and digital marketing — either leading a team that builds AI-powered marketing solutions, or architecting cloud-based ML systems that drive business decisions. I want to be in a role where my 18 years of marketing domain expertise informs my technical work, and vice versa."

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> "I've accumulated a very broad skill set across marketing, cloud, and AI — but this breadth sometimes makes it harder to project deep specialization in a single area. I've been addressing this by focusing my certification path on Google Cloud AI/ML and by prioritizing roles that let me go deep in that specific intersection, rather than trying to maintain expertise across every domain simultaneously."

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
